Restaurant Website Audit Checklist

Use this before buying ads, paying for a redesign, or pushing more traffic to a site that may be leaking diners.

Why this version is restaurant-focused

DC is a dense, visitor-heavy restaurant market: National Restaurant Association data lists 2,679 DC restaurant locations and 67,800 restaurant and foodservice jobs, while Destination DC reported $3.1B in 2024 visitor food-and-beverage spending. With local operators under margin pressure, the checklist focuses on the web decisions that affect calls, reservations, direct orders, reviews, and repeat visits.

Google, Maps, and reviews

  • Business name, address, phone, hours, and website match Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp, TripAdvisor, and social profiles.
  • Google Business Profile categories, menu link, reservation link, order link, photos, attributes, and holiday hours are current.
  • The website shows address, neighborhood, phone, hours, and a map or directions link without making visitors hunt.
  • Recent reviews are easy to find, and the site does not contradict what diners see on Google.

Menu, ordering, and reservations

  • The menu is readable on mobile without forcing a PDF download, tiny text, or a slow third-party embed.
  • Order, reserve, call, and directions actions are visible above the fold and repeated near the menu.
  • Direct ordering or reservation links are clearly labeled, so guests do not default to a marketplace or leave to search again.
  • Dietary notes, fees, delivery radius, pickup instructions, and catering/private-event options are clear where relevant.

Mobile speed and first impression

  • The first screen loads quickly on a phone and shows cuisine, location, hours, and the best next action.
  • Hero photos are compressed, current, and appetizing without blocking the menu, phone number, or order link.
  • Buttons are easy to tap, popups do not block the main action, and forms work on small screens.
  • The homepage title and search preview include the restaurant name, cuisine, and neighborhood or city.

Trust for locals and visitors

  • Photos show the real dining room, food, storefront, and team instead of generic stock images.
  • Parking, Metro/transit, accessibility, dress code, kid-friendliness, outdoor seating, and large-party notes are listed when useful.
  • Social links go to active profiles, and specials or events match what appears on Instagram, Facebook, or Google posts.
  • The footer has a current year, real contact details, and no broken links.

Measurement and margin

  • Analytics tracks menu views, order clicks, reservation clicks, phone taps, form submits, catering inquiries, and event requests.
  • Google Business Profile, Instagram, QR menus, link hub, email, and ads use tagged links so traffic sources are visible.
  • Third-party marketplace links are measured separately from direct order links.
  • One weekly log tracks calls, bookings, orders, review count, average rating, and top website fixes completed.

Fix first this week

  • If guests cannot find the menu, fix that first.
  • If they cannot order, reserve, call, or get directions in one tap, fix that second.
  • If Google details, hours, or reviews conflict with the website, fix that before sending more traffic.
  • If nothing is tracked, add basic analytics before judging whether the site works.
